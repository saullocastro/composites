Transverse shear stiffness and shear correction#

The FSDT gives transverse shear strains constant through the thickness, whereas the three-dimensional equilibrium requires transverse shear stresses that vary through the thickness and vanish at the traction-free faces. The FSDT accepts the inconsistency and corrects it in an average sense: its transverse shear stiffness \(A_{ts}\) is chosen smaller than the constant-strain one, such that the constant distribution stores the right energy, or reproduces another chosen response, for the same shear forces. This chapter derives the corrections implemented by Laminate.calc_transverse_shear_stiffness(), called at the end of Laminate.calc_constitutive_matrix(), compares them and guides the choice. The conventions are those of Laminate stiffness and plate theories: \(Q = \{Q_y, Q_x\}\), \(\gamma = \{\gamma_{yz}, \gamma_{xz}\}\), \(A_{ts} = [[A_{44}, A_{45}], [A_{45}, A_{55}]]\), with \(C_s^{(k)}\) of Eq. (6), in general a full matrix.

The correction factor#

In the simplest case the corrected stiffness is

(1)#\[A_{ts} = \kappa \bar{A}_{ts}, \qquad \bar{A}_{ts} = \sum_k \int_{z_k}^{z_{k+1}} C_s^{(k)} dz\]

For a homogeneous plate of shear modulus \(G\), the equilibrium of the linear bending stresses gives the parabola \(\tau_{xz} = 3 Q_x/(2h) (1 - 4 \bar{z}^2/h^2)\), \(\bar{z}\) measured from the mid-surface, whose complementary energy per unit area is \(\int \tau_{xz}^2/(2G) dz = 3 Q_x^2/(5 G h)\). Equating it with the energy \(Q_x^2/(2 \kappa G h)\) of the constant distribution gives \(\kappa = 5/6\). The factor is not a material property but the answer to a chosen criterion: matching instead the frequency of the first thickness-shear mode gives \(\kappa = \pi^2/12\) (Mindlin [8]). For a laminate the stress distribution is piecewise parabolic, continuous at the interfaces with slope discontinuities, the energy equivalence depends on the stacking sequence and on the direction, and the correction becomes a pair of factors or a \(2 \times 2\) matrix.

Three transverse shear stiffnesses are distinguished, following Rohwer [1], all computed with \(z\) measured from the mid-surface, such that none depends on offset:

(2)#\[\bar{A}_{ts} = \sum_k C_s^{(k)} h_k, \qquad \bar{\bar{A}}_{ts} = h^2 \left[ \sum_k \left( C_s^{(k)} \right)^{-1} h_k \right]^{-1}, \qquad A_{ts} = \text{corrected}\]

  • \(\bar{A}_{ts}\), Laminate.Abar_ts, constant strains through the thickness, the uncorrected stiffness used by the TSDT;

  • \(\bar{\bar{A}}_{ts}\), Laminate.Abarbar_ts, constant stresses \(Q/h\) with the same complementary energy, for comparison only, nan when a ply has a singular \(C_s^{(k)}\);

  • \(A_{ts}\), Laminate.Ats, A44, A45, A55, with the correction selected by shear_correction already applied, the stiffness of an FSDT model as it is.

Neither \(\bar{A}_{ts}\) nor \(\bar{\bar{A}}_{ts}\) gives any reduction for a homogeneous plate, and both overestimate the transverse shear stiffness of a laminate.

Equilibrium approach ('rohwer')#

The default method, Rohwer [1], derives the stresses from the equilibrium of two cylindrical bending states with constant membrane forces: one in which the resultants depend on \(x\) only, with \(M_{xx,x} = Q_x\) and \(M_{xy,x} = Q_y\), and one in which they depend on \(y\) only, with \(M_{yy,y} = Q_y\) and \(M_{xy,y} = Q_x\). With \(H^* = [[A, B], [B, D]]^{-1}\) about the mid-surface, the in-plane strains of a state are \((H^*_{1:3} + z H^*_{4:6}) \{N, M\}\), \(H^*_{1:3}\) and \(H^*_{4:6}\) being the first and the last three rows of \(H^*\). Integrating \(\tau_{xz,z} = -\sigma_{xx,x}\) and \(\tau_{yz,z} = -\sigma_{yy,y}\) from the bottom face, with the rows \(c_1^{(k)}\), \(c_2^{(k)}\) of \(C^{(k)}\) that give \(\sigma_{xx}\), \(\sigma_{yy}\) and \(\Pi(z) = z H^*_{1:3} + z^2/2 \, H^*_{4:6}\):

(3)#\[\tau_{xz} = \left( a_1^{(k)} - c_1^{(k)} \Pi(z) \right) L_x Q, \qquad \tau_{yz} = \left( a_2^{(k)} - c_2^{(k)} \Pi(z) \right) L_y Q, \qquad a_\alpha^{(k)} = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \left( c_\alpha^{(i)} - c_\alpha^{(i-1)} \right) \Pi(z_i)\]

for \(z_k \le z \le z_{k+1}\), with \(c_\alpha^{(0)} = 0\), where \(L_x\) places \(Q_y\), \(Q_x\) at the components \(M_{xy}\), \(M_{xx}\) of \(\{N, M\}_{,x}\) and \(L_y\) places them at \(M_{yy}\), \(M_{xy}\) of \(\{N, M\}_{,y}\). The sums \(a_\alpha^{(k)}\) make the stresses continuous at the interfaces, and the stresses vanish at the top face because \(\int c_1 (H^*_{1:3} + z H^*_{4:6}) dz\) through the thickness is the row of \(N_{xx}\) of the identity, which \(L_x\) does not select, and analogously for \(c_2\). The result is the distribution \(\{\tau_{yz}, \tau_{xz}\} = f^{(k)}(z) Q\), quadratic within each ply, and the stiffness follows from the complementary energy:

(4)#\[A_{ts} = \left[ \sum_k \int_{z_k}^{z_{k+1}} f^{(k)T} \left(C_s^{(k)}\right)^{-1} f^{(k)} dz \right]^{-1}\]

integrated exactly with three Gauss-Legendre points per ply, the integrand being of degree 4. The method admits general anisotropic and unsymmetric laminates, angle plies with full \(C^{(k)}\) and \(C_s^{(k)}\) included, and gives the coupling \(A_{45}\) from the bending-twisting terms \(D_{16}\), \(D_{26}\) through \(M_{xy,x} = Q_y\), also in balanced laminates, for which \(\bar{A}_{45} = 0\). It gives \(\kappa = 5/6\) for a homogeneous plate.

The two states are tied to the axes \(x\), \(y\), such that the result is not invariant to a rotation of the axes, which is inherent to the method (Rohwer [1], Fig. 7, p. 20): for his [0,90]_s CFRP laminate of Table 2, computed in axes rotated by 45 degrees, A44 = A55 are 7.0 % and A45 is 13.5 % above the tensor rotation of the result in the axes of the plies. A laminate whose material directions are rotated with respect to the axes of a model is therefore created with the ply angles shifted by the rotation, which gives the stiffness in the axes of the model, rather than rotating Ats. The distribution \(f^{(k)}(z)\) is stored and gives the transverse shear stresses of Transverse shear stresses and a posteriori energies.

Scalar factors per direction#

The other methods give one factor per direction, \(k_{13}\) for \(xz\) and \(k_{23}\) for \(yz\), applied to the constant-strain stiffness as

(5)#\[A_{55} = k_{13} \bar{A}_{55}, \qquad A_{44} = k_{23} \bar{A}_{44}, \qquad A_{45} = \frac{k_{13} + k_{23}}{2} \bar{A}_{45}\]

The averaged \(A_{45}\) is ad hoc, not the result of an energy equivalence, and vanishes whenever \(\bar{A}_{45} = 0\), e.g. in every balanced laminate.

  • 'vlachoutsis', Vlachoutsis [2]: the laminate is treated in each direction as a beam in cylindrical bending, with \(C_{11} = q_{11L}\) and \(C_{55} = q_{55L}\) of each ply in the direction 1, and the one-dimensional equilibrium \(\tau_{xz,z} = -\sigma_{xx,x}\) about the direction-wise neutral surface \(z_n\):

    (6)#\[z_n = \frac{\int C_{11} z \, dz}{\int C_{11} dz}, \quad R = \int C_{11} (z - z_n)^2 dz, \quad g(z) = -\int_{z_1}^{z} C_{11} (\bar{z} - z_n) d\bar{z}, \quad k_{13} = \frac{R^2}{\bar{A}_{55} \int g^2/C_{55} \, dz}\]

    i.e. the energy of \(\tau_{xz} = Q_x g(z)/R\) equated with \(Q_x^2/(2 k_{13} \bar{A}_{55})\); analogously \(k_{23}\) with \(q_{22L}\), \(q_{44L}\) and \(\bar{A}_{44}\). The scalar \(C_{\alpha\alpha}\) per direction ignores the Poisson coupling, the shear-extension coupling of angle plies and \(C_{45}\), such that the factors are exact only for specially orthotropic plies. Vlachoutsis also gives closed forms for sandwiches.

  • 'whitney', Whitney [3], Eqs. (3)-(7), extending Chow to unsymmetric orthotropic laminates in cylindrical bending: the stress gradient \(\sigma_{xx,x} = C_{11} (z - z_n) Q_x/R\), with \(z_n = B_{11}/A_{11}\) and \(R = (D_{11} A_{11} - B_{11}^2)/A_{11}\), is that of 'vlachoutsis', and the result is identical.

  • 'chow', Chow [4], Eqs. (11)-(12): the same with \(z\) measured from the mid-surface, \(g(z) = -\int C_{11} \bar{z} d\bar{z}\) and \(k_{13} = D_{11}^2/(\bar{A}_{55} \int g^2/C_{55} dz)\), valid for laminates symmetric about the mid-surface only, for which it is identical to 'vlachoutsis'; an unsymmetric laminate raises ValueError.

  • 'birman_bert', Birman and Bert [5], Eq. (13) and Appendix B: the thickness integral of the transverse shear strain of the equilibrium distribution, instead of its energy, is equated with that of the FSDT, \(k_{13} = R h/(\bar{A}_{55} \int g/C_{55} dz)\), with \(g\) and \(R\) of 'vlachoutsis'. It gives \(k = 1\) for a homogeneous plate.

  • 'thickness_shear', Yang, Norris and Stavsky [6], as described by Sun and Whitney [7], extending the criterion of Mindlin [8]: the frequency of the first thickness-shear mode of the FSDT, \(\omega^2 = k_{13} \bar{A}_{55}/\bar{I}_2\), is matched with the exact frequency of the anti-plane motion of the laminate, \(\rho^{(k)} \ddot{u} = (C_{55}^{(k)} u_{,z})_{,z}\) with traction-free faces, computed with the exact transfer matrix of each ply. \(\bar{I}_2 = I_2 - I_1^2/I_0\) is the rotatory inertia about the centre of mass, see Thickness integrals for mass matrices, and \(C_{45}\) is neglected. It gives \(k = \pi^2/12\) for a homogeneous plate and requires positive ply densities.

  • 'constant': \(k = 5/6\) for any laminate, A_ts = 5/6 Abar_ts.

  • None: no correction, Ats = Abar_ts.

The ratios scf_k13 = A55/Abar55 and scf_k23 = A44/Abar44 are stored for every method. They are informative only: the correction is already inside Ats and must not be applied again. They allow the methods to be compared, or report the equivalent scalar factors of 'rohwer'.

'rohwer' and 'vlachoutsis' integrate the same equilibrium equation in cylindrical bending. For symmetric cross-ply laminates, where the couplings that 'vlachoutsis' ignores are small, they nearly coincide; for unsymmetric and angle-ply laminates only 'rohwer' accounts for the full laminate compliance and gives a consistent \(A_{45}\), which is why it is the default. The scalar methods remain useful to reproduce published factors, to compare with closed forms of sandwiches, and when one factor per direction is required. 'thickness_shear' is the dynamic criterion, appropriate when the thickness-shear frequencies matter, and 'birman_bert' the criterion of the average strain discussed by its authors for sandwiches; the value they recommend for sandwich structures, \(K = 1\) applied to \(\bar{A}_{55}\), is None.

The choice matters in proportion to the transverse shear compliance: little for thin plates, decisively for thick laminates and sandwiches with a soft core, whose factors may be far below 5/6. For the [0,90]_s CFRP plate of Rohwer [1], Sec. 4.2, simply supported, with \(a/b = 1.5\) and a distributed load, the transverse shear influence on the centre deflection reaches 10 % at \(a/h = 19\) and exceeds 30 % at \(a/h = 11\) (p. 21, Fig. 8). At \(a/h = 10\), “the Mindlin displacement exceeds the Kirchhoff one by 25% and 28% for Hbar and Hbarbar, respectively, whereas with H it is 36% higher” (p. 21), \(H\) being his equilibrium stiffness.

A model that requires its own factors, e.g. from a reference solution, applies them to the uncorrected matrix, \(A_{ts} = \kappa \bar{A}_{ts}\) or Eq. (5) with lam.Abar44, lam.Abar45, lam.Abar55. Laminates created from lamination parameters have no plies, hence no through-thickness distribution: their Ats is Abar_ts and shear_correction is None, see Lamination parameters and double-double laminates. The method is chosen with the argument shear_correction of laminated_plate() and isotropic_plate(), or by changing the attribute shear_correction and calling Laminate.calc_transverse_shear_stiffness() again.

All methods except 'constant' and None raise ValueError for a ply with a singular \(C_s^{(k)}\), e.g. \(G_{13} = 0\); 'rohwer' also for a singular ABD matrix, and the scalar methods for zero in-plane stiffness in a direction.

The methods assume static states of the laminate alone. Factors of an actual FSDT solution, in the sense of Noor and Peters [9], follow from the recovered stresses of Transverse shear stresses and a posteriori energies.

Verification#

tests/test_transverse_shear.py compares the methods with the values printed in the references, to half a unit of their last digit, e.g. the [0,90]_s laminate of Rohwer [1], Table 2, p. 19, the cross-ply laminates of 120 plies and the isotropic sandwiches of Vlachoutsis [2], the laminates of Whitney [3] and Chow [4], and the sandwich beams of Birman and Bert [5]; with closed forms, such as the factor of a symmetrical sandwich of Vlachoutsis [2], Eqs. (44), (45) and (48), and, for an antiplane core, the shear stiffness \(G_c d^2/c\) of Allen [10], Eq. (2.15b), p. 17; and with an independent implementation of Rohwer’s equations for random laminates, for which the results are also checked to be independent of offset.

Some printed values are not reproduced, and are checked against the closed forms of their papers instead, as stated in the Notes of the tests. Chow [4], Table 1, p. 316, gives \(k_2^2 = 0.521\) for the graphite-epoxy [0,90]_s plate, whereas his Eqs. (11)-(12), p. 311, give 0.52165 with his data, Eq. (32), which reproduce the three other values of the table. Whitney [3], p. 304, gives \(k_1^2 = 0.4098\) and \(k_2^2 = 0.6915\) for a sandwich, whereas his Eqs. (3)-(7), p. 303, give 0.40936 and 0.67199 with his data. The sandwich panel of Rohwer [1], Sec. 4.3, Table 4, p. 23, is not used: its transverse shear stiffnesses are reproduced only with a foam shear modulus of 0.04 kN/mm2, i.e. \(\nu = 0.25\) instead of the stated 0.3, and its bending stiffnesses contradict the stated lay-up.
