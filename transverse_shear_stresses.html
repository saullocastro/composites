Transverse shear stresses and a posteriori energies#

The shear correction of Transverse shear stiffness and shear correction makes the FSDT store the right transverse shear energy, but not the right stresses: \(C_s^{(k)} \gamma^{(0)}\) is constant within each ply, discontinuous at the interfaces and non-zero at the free faces, where the tractions are known to vanish. When \(\tau_{xz}\) and \(\tau_{yz}\) are needed, e.g. for an interlaminar failure criterion, they are recovered by integrating the three-dimensional equilibrium equations through the thickness, from the in-plane stresses of the plies. Laminate offers two recoveries: from the shear forces, with the distribution of the equilibrium approach, and from the strain gradients of an actual solution.

From the shear forces#

Laminate.calc_transverse_shear_stress() evaluates, at a height \(z\) from the reference surface, the distribution of Rohwer [1], Eq. (3):

(1)#\[\begin{split}\begin{Bmatrix} \tau_{yz} \\ \tau_{xz} \end{Bmatrix} = f^{(k)}(z) \begin{Bmatrix} Q_y \\ Q_x \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

for the shear forces of the FSDT, e.g. \(Q = A_{ts} \gamma^{(0)}\). The distribution is quadratic within each ply, continuous at the interfaces, zero at both faces, and its resultants are \(Q\). It depends only on the in-plane stiffnesses of the plies, through the inverse of the ABD matrix, and not on shear_correction: it is stored by Laminate.calc_transverse_shear_stiffness() with 'rohwer' and computed on the first call otherwise, and it does not depend on offset, being computed about the mid-surface. For a homogeneous plate it is the parabola \(\tau_{xz} = 3 Q_x/(2h) (1 - 4 \bar{z}^2/h^2)\), \(\bar{z}\) measured from the mid-surface.

The distribution assumes the two cylindrical bending states of Rohwer, i.e. constant membrane forces and the moment gradients \(M_{xx,x} = Q_x\), \(M_{xy,x} = Q_y\), \(M_{yy,y} = Q_y\), \(M_{xy,y} = Q_x\). It is the natural recovery when only the shear forces of a model are known.

From the strain gradients#

When the derivatives of the generalized strains are available, e.g. from the shape functions of a finite element or of a Ritz solution, Laminate.calc_equilibrium_transverse_shear() integrates the equilibrium of the actual in-plane stresses \(\sigma^{(k)} = C^{(k)} (\varepsilon^{(0)} + z \varepsilon^{(1)})\) from the bottom face \(z_1\):

(2)#\[\begin{split}\tau_{xz}(z) = -\int_{z_1}^{z} \left( c_1 \left[ \varepsilon^{(0)}_{,x} + \bar{z} \varepsilon^{(1)}_{,x} \right] + c_3 \left[ \varepsilon^{(0)}_{,y} + \bar{z} \varepsilon^{(1)}_{,y} \right] \right) d\bar{z} \\ \tau_{yz}(z) = -\int_{z_1}^{z} \left( c_2 \left[ \varepsilon^{(0)}_{,y} + \bar{z} \varepsilon^{(1)}_{,y} \right] + c_3 \left[ \varepsilon^{(0)}_{,x} + \bar{z} \varepsilon^{(1)}_{,x} \right] \right) d\bar{z}\end{split}\]

i.e. \(\tau_{xz,z} = -(\sigma_{xx,x} + \tau_{xy,y})\) and \(\tau_{yz,z} = -(\tau_{xy,x} + \sigma_{yy,y})\), with \(c_1\), \(c_2\), \(c_3\) the rows of \(C^{(k)}\) that give \(\sigma_{xx}\), \(\sigma_{yy}\), \(\tau_{xy}\). The arguments eps_x and eps_y are the derivatives of \(\{\varepsilon^{(0)}, \varepsilon^{(1)}\}\) in the order of Laminate.ABD, at any number of points, and the stresses are returned at the heights z, by default the three Gauss-Legendre points of each ply. The integrals are exact, ply by ply. This is the a posteriori approach of Noor and Peters [2]: it uses the gradients of the solution instead of assumed states. The stresses vanish at the bottom face by construction, and at the top face only if the gradients satisfy the in-plane equilibrium of the stress resultants, \(N_{xx,x} + N_{xy,y} = 0\) and \(N_{xy,x} + N_{yy,y} = 0\), which an approximate solution does only approximately; the residual at the top face is a measure of that error. With the gradients of the two cylindrical bending states, the recovery gives back the distribution of Eq. (1).

A posteriori energies and factors#

Laminate.calc_aposteriori_energy() evaluates, with the recovered stresses and the strains \(\{\bar\gamma_{yz}, \bar\gamma_{xz}\} = (C_s^{(k)})^{-1} \{\bar\tau_{yz}, \bar\tau_{xz}\}\), the transverse shear strain energies per unit area of Noor and Peters [2], Eq. (7):

(3)#\[\bar{U}_{13} = \int \frac{1}{2} C_{55} \bar\gamma_{xz}^2 \, dz, \qquad \bar{U}_{23} = \int \frac{1}{2} C_{44} \bar\gamma_{yz}^2 \, dz\]

with \(C_{44} = q_{44L}\), \(C_{55} = q_{55L}\) of each ply, together with the resultants \(Q_y = \int \bar\tau_{yz} dz\), \(Q_x = \int \bar\tau_{xz} dz\) of the recovered stresses, integrated exactly with three Gauss-Legendre points per ply. Equating, over the domain of the model, the FSDT energy with a factor \(k_{13}\), \(Q_x^2/(2 k_{13} \bar{A}_{55})\), with the energy of the recovered stresses gives the a posteriori factor

(4)#\[k_{13} = \frac{\int Q_x^2/(2 \bar{A}_{55}) \, dA}{\int \bar{U}_{13} \, dA}\]

and analogously \(k_{23}\) with \(Q_y\), \(\bar{A}_{44}\) and \(\bar{U}_{23}\) (Noor and Peters [2], Eqs. (8)-(9)), where \(Q\) are the shear forces of the FSDT solution, obtained with an initial factor. For a homogeneous plate in cylindrical bending, \(\bar{U}_{13} = 3 Q_x^2/(5 G h)\) and \(k_{13} = 5/6\). Unlike the factors of Transverse shear stiffness and shear correction, these depend on the problem: on the loads, the boundary conditions and the position in the structure. A ply with a singular \(C_s^{(k)}\) raises ValueError.

Verification#

tests/test_transverse_shear.py checks that the stresses vanish at the faces, are continuous at the interfaces and integrate to the shear forces for random laminates, that they agree with an independent implementation of Rohwer’s equations, that they do not depend on offset or on shear_correction, that the recovery from the gradients of the two cylindrical bending states gives Eq. (1) with a traction-free top face, and the energy and the factor 5/6 of a homogeneous plate.
