Laminate stiffness and plate theories#

A laminate is modelled as an equivalent single layer: the displacements are assumed through the thickness in terms of functions of the reference surface alone, and the three-dimensional strain energy, integrated through the thickness, leaves the stiffness of the laminate as a set of thickness integrals of the ply stiffnesses of Ply constitutive relations and invariants. A Laminate contains the integrals required by three such theories: the classical laminated plate theory (CLPT), the first-order shear deformation theory (FSDT) and the third-order shear deformation theory (TSDT) of Reddy [1]. All of them are computed at once by Laminate.calc_constitutive_matrix(), which is called by laminated_plate() and isotropic_plate(), such that the same object serves a model of any of the three theories.

Displacement fields and generalized strains#

With \(z\) measured from the reference surface, the in-plane displacements of the three theories are [1]:

(1)#\[\begin{split}\text{CLPT:} \quad u = u_0 - z w_{,x}, \quad v = v_0 - z w_{,y} \\ \text{FSDT:} \quad u = u_0 + z \phi_x, \quad v = v_0 + z \phi_y \\ \text{TSDT:} \quad u = u_0 + z \phi_x - \frac{4}{3h^2} z^3 (\phi_x + w_{,x}), \quad v = v_0 + z \phi_y - \frac{4}{3h^2} z^3 (\phi_y + w_{,y})\end{split}\]

and \(w(x, y)\) in all of them, i.e. the transverse normal is inextensible, \(\varepsilon_{zz} = 0\). The CLPT keeps the normal straight and normal to the deformed reference surface; the FSDT keeps it straight but lets it rotate independently, with the rotations \(\phi_x\), \(\phi_y\); the TSDT lets it warp with a cubic term chosen such that the transverse shear strains vanish at \(z = \pm h/2\). The in-plane strains \(\varepsilon = \{\varepsilon_{xx}, \varepsilon_{yy}, \gamma_{xy}\}\) and the transverse shear strains \(\gamma = \{\gamma_{yz}, \gamma_{xz}\}\) are written with the generalized strains of the reference surface as:

(2)#\[\varepsilon = \varepsilon^{(0)} + z \varepsilon^{(1)} + z^3 \varepsilon^{(3)}, \qquad \gamma = \gamma^{(0)} + z^2 \gamma^{(2)}\]

where \(\varepsilon^{(3)}\) and \(\gamma^{(2)}\) exist only in the TSDT, and \(\gamma^{(0)}\) vanishes in the CLPT. The FSDT gives a constant \(\gamma^{(0)}\) through the thickness, which requires a shear correction, see Transverse shear stiffness and shear correction, whereas the TSDT satisfies the traction-free conditions at the faces and needs no shear correction.

Thickness integrals#

With \(C^{(k)}\) the plane-stress stiffness of ply \(k\) in the laminate axes and \(C_s^{(k)}\) its transverse shear stiffness, Eq. (6), and the ply occupying \(z_k \le z \le z_{k+1}\), from \(z_1 = -h/2 + d\) to \(z_{N+1} = h/2 + d\), \(d\) being offset, see Defining a laminate: conventions:

(3)#\[\begin{split}(A, B, D, E, F, H) = \sum_k \int_{z_k}^{z_{k+1}} C^{(k)} (1, z, z^2, z^3, z^4, z^6) \, dz \\ (\bar{A}_{ts}, D_{ts}, F_{ts}) = \sum_k \int_{z_k}^{z_{k+1}} C_s^{(k)} (1, z^2, z^4) \, dz\end{split}\]

integrated exactly, ply by ply, e.g. \(A_{ij} = \sum_k q_{ijL}^{(k)} (z_{k+1} - z_k)\), \(B_{ij} = \sum_k q_{ijL}^{(k)} (z_{k+1}^2 - z_k^2)/2\) and \(D_{ij} = \sum_k q_{ijL}^{(k)} (z_{k+1}^3 - z_k^3)/3\). The FSDT does not use \(\bar{A}_{ts}\) directly but the corrected \(A_{ts}\), see Laminate.calc_transverse_shear_stiffness() and Transverse shear stiffness and shear correction. The moments of the density through the thickness, computed in the same loop, are described in Thickness integrals for mass matrices.

Constitutive relations#

The stress resultants \(N\), \(M\), \(P\) are the integrals of \(\{\sigma_{xx}, \sigma_{yy}, \tau_{xy}\}\) times \(1\), \(z\) and \(z^3\), and \(Q\), \(R\) those of \(\{\tau_{yz}, \tau_{xz}\}\) times \(1\) and \(z^2\), such that \(Q = \{Q_y, Q_x\}\). The strain energy per unit area of the reference surface is half the product of the resultants by the generalized strains, and the constitutive relations become:

(4)#\[\begin{split}\text{CLPT:} \quad \begin{Bmatrix} N \\ M \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ B & D \end{bmatrix} \begin{Bmatrix} \varepsilon^{(0)} \\ \varepsilon^{(1)} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]
(5)#\[\begin{split}\text{FSDT:} \quad \begin{Bmatrix} N \\ M \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ B & D \end{bmatrix} \begin{Bmatrix} \varepsilon^{(0)} \\ \varepsilon^{(1)} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad Q = A_{ts} \gamma^{(0)}\end{split}\]
(6)#\[\begin{split}\text{TSDT:} \quad \begin{Bmatrix} N \\ M \\ P \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} A & B & E \\ B & D & F \\ E & F & H \end{bmatrix} \begin{Bmatrix} \varepsilon^{(0)} \\ \varepsilon^{(1)} \\ \varepsilon^{(3)} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \begin{Bmatrix} Q \\ R \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \bar{A}_{ts} & D_{ts} \\ D_{ts} & F_{ts} \end{bmatrix} \begin{Bmatrix} \gamma^{(0)} \\ \gamma^{(2)} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

The transverse shear matrices are ordered as [[44, 45], [45, 55]], with the index 4 corresponding to \(yz\) and the index 5 to \(xz\), in the same order as \(\gamma = \{\gamma_{yz}, \gamma_{xz}\}\) and \(Q = \{Q_y, Q_x\}\). Some references, e.g. Rohwer [2], use the opposite order \(\{xz, yz\}\).

The subscripts 16, 26 and 66 refer to the \(xy\) components. In the transverse shear blocks of the TSDT, some references denote \(\bar{A}_{ts}\), \(D_{ts}\) and \(F_{ts}\) with the same letters \(A\), \(D\) and \(F\) used for the in-plane stiffnesses; in composites they have their own names.

Reference surface#

All the integrals are taken with \(z\) measured from the reference surface, the surface of the generalized strains. Shifting the reference surface by \(d\) from the mid-surface changes the odd and the higher integrals, but not \(A\) nor the transverse shear stiffness \(\bar{A}_{ts}\): with the superscript 0 for the integrals about the mid-surface (Vasiliev and Morozov [3], Eq. (5.28), p. 235),

(7)#\[B = B^0 + d A, \qquad D = D^0 + 2 d B^0 + d^2 A\]

such that \(B \ne 0\) for any laminate with an offset, \(B = d h C\) for an isotropic plate of plane-stress stiffness \(C\). The equivalent properties, their conditioning check and the shear corrections are evaluated about the mid-surface, which makes them independent of offset and avoids the round-off of large offsets.

Special laminates#

Since \(q_{16L}\), \(q_{26L}\) and \(q_{45L}\) are odd functions of the ply angle and the other terms are even, see Ply constitutive relations and invariants, the stacking sequence makes some integrals vanish. Four methods impose these simplifications on the stored stiffnesses, all of them requiring offset = 0:

  • symmetric laminate, Laminate.make_symmetric(): the integrands of the odd powers of \(z\) are odd about the mid-surface, \(B = E = 0\);

  • balanced laminate, with a ply at \(-\theta\) of the same material and thickness for every ply at \(+\theta\), anywhere in the stack, Laminate.make_balanced(): only the integrals of \(z^0\) of the odd terms vanish, \(A_{16} = A_{26} = \bar{A}_{45} = \bar{\bar{A}}_{45} = 0\), whereas \(B_{16}\), \(D_{16}\), …, \(D_{45}\), \(F_{45}\) depend on the positions of the plies and are kept. A45 is also set to zero, except with the shear correction 'rohwer', whose \(A_{45}\) follows from the bending-twisting coupling and does not vanish in general, see Transverse shear stiffness and shear correction;

  • orthotropic laminate, all plies at 0 or 90 degrees, Laminate.make_orthotropic(): every integral of \(q_{16L}\), \(q_{26L}\) and \(q_{45L}\) vanishes, i.e. the terms 16 and 26 of \(A\) to \(H\) and the terms 45 of the transverse shear matrices;

  • smeared laminate, the limit of many repetitions of the stacking sequence, Laminate.make_smeared(): \(B = 0\) and \(D = h^2 A/12\), the stiffnesses of a homogeneous plate with the in-plane stiffness \(A/h\). The double-double and N-double laminates of Lamination parameters and double-double laminates are built with this assumption.

The same simplifications of the lamination parameters are made by make_symmetric_LP(), make_balanced_LP() and make_orthotropic_LP().

Equivalent engineering properties#

Laminate.calc_equivalent_properties(), called by laminated_plate(), gives the membrane moduli of the laminate with zero moments and free curvatures, from \(a = [[A, B], [B, D]]^{-1}\):

(8)#\[E_1 = \frac{1}{h a_{11}}, \quad E_2 = \frac{1}{h a_{22}}, \quad G_{12} = \frac{1}{h a_{66}}, \quad \nu_{12} = -\frac{a_{12}}{a_{11}}, \quad \nu_{21} = -\frac{a_{12}}{a_{22}}\]

where the index 6 is the third row and column of \(a\), stored in e1, e2, g12, nu12 and nu21 of the Laminate. For an unsymmetric laminate these are not the moduli of \(A^{-1}\), since the curvatures are free. They are undefined when the ABD matrix is singular, e.g. for a laminate of antiplane cores only, or for a single ply at an angle with \(G_{12} = 0\), whose ABD has a positive diagonal and is singular nevertheless. The method raises numpy.linalg.LinAlgError when the diagonal is not positive, when the inverse is not finite, or when the 1-norm condition number of the matrix about the mid-surface, scaled by its diagonal such that it does not depend on the units, exceeds \(10^{12}\).

Choosing the theory#

The three theories use the same in-plane integrals \(A\), \(B\), \(D\) and differ in the treatment of the transverse shear:

  • the CLPT neglects the transverse shear deformation and needs only \(A\), \(B\), \(D\). It is the theory of thin laminates, and the one of the closed forms of Buckling of rectangular plates (Kassapoglou);

  • the FSDT adds \(A_{ts}\), with the accuracy of the shear correction. The transverse shear compliance relative to the bending compliance grows with \((h/L)^2\), \(L\) being an in-plane length, and with the ratio of the in-plane to the transverse shear moduli, such that “in CFRP laminates the transverse shear effect must be accounted for up to higher slenderness rates than in homogeneous structures” (Rohwer [2], p. 24), and in sandwiches always;

  • the TSDT represents the parabolic variation of the transverse shear strains and needs no correction, at the cost of the additional generalized strains \(\varepsilon^{(3)}\), \(\gamma^{(2)}\) and of the matrices \(E\), \(F\), \(H\), \(D_{ts}\), \(F_{ts}\). Its displacement field, Eq. (1), assumes the reference surface at the mid-surface for the traction-free conditions at \(\pm h/2\), whereas all the matrices of a Laminate are integrated about the reference surface, including offset.

Laminates created from lamination parameters, or as double-double or N-double laminates, have only part of these stiffnesses, see Lamination parameters and double-double laminates.

Assembling the matrices in a model#

A finite element or Ritz model gathers the generalized strains in one vector \(e\) and the blocks above in one symmetric matrix \(K_c\), such that the strain energy per unit area is \(e^T K_c e / 2\) and the stiffness matrix is \(\int B^T K_c B \, dA\), where \(B\) relates the degrees of freedom to \(e\). The common layouts are:

  • CLPT, 6 x 6: \(e = \{\varepsilon^{(0)}, \varepsilon^{(1)}\}\), \(K_c =\) lam.ABD.

  • FSDT, 8 x 8: \(e = \{\varepsilon^{(0)}, \varepsilon^{(1)}, \gamma^{(0)}\}\), K_c = [[ABD, 0], [0, Ats]].

  • TSDT, 13 x 13: \(e = \{\varepsilon^{(0)}, \varepsilon^{(1)}, \varepsilon^{(3)}, \gamma^{(0)}, \gamma^{(2)}\}\), K_c = [[A, B, E, 0, 0], [B, D, F, 0, 0], [E, F, H, 0, 0], [0, 0, 0, Abar_ts, Dts], [0, 0, 0, Dts, Fts]].

These are, for instance, the layouts of the Ritz models of the panels library. In an FSDT model with a shear correction factor of the user’s choice, the factor is applied to Abar_ts instead of using Ats, see Transverse shear stiffness and shear correction.
