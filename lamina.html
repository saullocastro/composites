Ply constitutive relations and invariants#

Every stiffness of a laminate is a thickness integral of the stiffnesses of its plies. This chapter describes how composites obtains them: from the engineering constants of the material, MatLamina, to the plane-stress stiffnesses of a ply rotated to the laminate axes, Lamina, and to the material invariants on which the lamination parameters of Lamination parameters and double-double laminates are built.

Material axes and engineering constants#

The material axes of a ply are 1, the fibre direction, 2, the in-plane direction transverse to the fibres, and 3, the normal to the ply. An orthotropic material is described by the Young moduli \(E_1\), \(E_2\), \(E_3\), the shear moduli \(G_{12}\), \(G_{13}\), \(G_{23}\) and the Poisson’s ratios \(\nu_{12}\), \(\nu_{13}\), \(\nu_{23}\), the attributes e1 to nu23 of MatLamina. The other three Poisson’s ratios follow from the reciprocity relations \(\nu_{ij}/E_i = \nu_{ji}/E_j\), which make the compliance matrix symmetric [1], and are computed by read_laminaprop():

(1)#\[\nu_{21} = \nu_{12} \frac{E_2}{E_1}, \qquad \nu_{31} = \nu_{13} \frac{E_3}{E_1}, \qquad \nu_{32} = \nu_{23} \frac{E_3}{E_2}\]

read_laminaprop() accepts 2, 6 or 9 constants, see Defining a laminate: conventions: with 2, (E, nu), the material is isotropic with \(G = E/(2(1 + \nu))\); with 6 or 9 the shear moduli are taken as given, and MatLamina never recomputes a modulus from the others. With 2 or 6 constants \(E_3 = \nu_{13} = \nu_{23} = 0\).

The constants must be those of a positive-definite material (Jones [2], Sec. 2.4.2): \(E_i \ge 0\) and \(G_{ij} \ge 0\), Eq. (2.45), \(|\nu_{ij}| < (E_i/E_j)^{1/2}\) for \(E_j > 0\), Eq. (2.51), and

(2)#\[\Delta = 1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21} - \nu_{23}\nu_{32} - \nu_{31}\nu_{13} - 2\nu_{21}\nu_{32}\nu_{13} > 0\]

Eq. (2.48). MatLamina.rebuild() raises ValueError naming the violated conditions. The strict inequalities of Jones are relaxed to admit zero moduli as the limit of a positive-definite material, in which the stiffnesses stay finite while the compliances diverge. Two limits are useful in practice:

  • the antiplane core of a sandwich, \(E_1 = E_2 = G_{12} = 0\) with finite \(G_{13}\), \(G_{23}\), “an idealized core in which the modulus of elasticity in planes parallel with the faces is zero but the shear modulus in planes perpendicular to the faces is finite” (Allen [3], p. 14), which adds only transverse shear stiffness to a laminate. With \(E_i = 0 < E_j\) the bound above requires \(\nu_{ij} = 0\), and read_laminaprop() stores \(\nu_{ji} = 0\);

  • a ply with a singular transverse shear stiffness, \(G_{13} = 0\) or \(G_{23} = 0\), which is accepted by the material but rejected by every shear correction except 'constant' and None, see Transverse shear stiffness and shear correction.

Three-dimensional stiffness#

The stiffness constants c11 to c66 of MatLamina are those of the three-dimensional orthotropic material, the inverse of the compliance matrix with \(S_{11} = 1/E_1\), \(S_{12} = -\nu_{12}/E_1\), \(S_{13} = -\nu_{13}/E_1\), \(S_{22} = 1/E_2\), \(S_{23} = -\nu_{23}/E_2\), \(S_{33} = 1/E_3\), \(S_{44} = 1/G_{23}\), \(S_{55} = 1/G_{13}\), \(S_{66} = 1/G_{12}\) [1]. With \(\Delta\) of Eq. (2):

(3)#\[\begin{split}C_{11} = \frac{E_1 (1 - \nu_{23}\nu_{32})}{\Delta}, \quad C_{12} = \frac{E_1 (\nu_{21} + \nu_{31}\nu_{23})}{\Delta}, \quad C_{13} = \frac{E_1 (\nu_{31} + \nu_{21}\nu_{32})}{\Delta} \\ C_{22} = \frac{E_2 (1 - \nu_{13}\nu_{31})}{\Delta}, \quad C_{23} = \frac{E_2 (\nu_{32} + \nu_{12}\nu_{31})}{\Delta}, \quad C_{33} = \frac{E_3 (1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21})}{\Delta} \\ C_{44} = G_{23}, \quad C_{55} = G_{13}, \quad C_{66} = G_{12}\end{split}\]

MatLamina.get_constitutive_matrix() returns this 6 x 6 matrix in the order \(\{11, 22, 33, 23, 13, 12\}\). With a zero \(E_1\) or \(E_2\) the same constants are computed without dividing by the moduli, which keeps them finite, e.g. an antiplane core given with 9 constants (0, 0, nu12, 0, G13, G23, E3, 0, 0) has \(C_{33} = E_3\), \(C_{44} = G_{23}\), \(C_{55} = G_{13}\) and all other constants zero.

Plane stress#

The plate and shell theories of Laminate stiffness and plate theories neglect the normal stress, \(\sigma_{33} = 0\). Eliminating \(\varepsilon_{33}\) from the three-dimensional relation gives the reduced stiffnesses \(Q_{ij} = C_{ij} - C_{i3} C_{j3}/C_{33}\), \(i, j = 1, 2\) (Jones [2], Eq. (2.64)), which depend on six constants only [1] [2]:

(4)#\[\begin{split}Q_{11} = \frac{E_1}{1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21}}, \quad Q_{12} = \frac{\nu_{12} E_2}{1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21}}, \quad Q_{22} = \frac{E_2}{1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21}} \\ Q_{66} = G_{12}, \quad Q_{44} = G_{23}, \quad Q_{55} = G_{13}\end{split}\]

The transverse shear stiffnesses \(Q_{44}\), \(Q_{55}\) are kept, since the FSDT and the TSDT use them; \(E_3\), \(\nu_{13}\) and \(\nu_{23}\) do not enter. These are the stiffnesses that Lamina.rebuild() rotates and that MatLamina.rebuild() stores in q11 to q66 with its default plane_stress=True, also when 9 constants are given. With rebuild(plane_stress=False), q11 to q33 and the invariants become the three-dimensional \(C_{ij}\) of Eq. (3), which are stiffer, e.g. \(E(1 - \nu)/((1 + \nu)(1 - 2\nu))\) instead of \(E/(1 - \nu^2)\) for an isotropic material: they are meant for three-dimensional models, such as continuum shell elements, and not for the laminate stiffnesses of Laminate. The read-only attribute plane_stress records the choice. Thermal expansion is not considered in the stiffnesses; the strength allowables st1, sc1, ss12, etc. are stored only.

Rotation to the laminate axes#

The ply angle \(\theta\) is measured from the laminate axis \(x\) to the material direction 1, positive from \(x\) towards \(y\), see Defining a laminate: conventions. With \(c = \cos\theta\) and \(s = \sin\theta\), the plane-stress stiffnesses of the ply in the laminate axes, the attributes q11L to q55L computed by Lamina.rebuild(), are [1]:

(5)#\[\begin{split}q_{11L} = Q_{11} c^4 + 2 (Q_{12} + 2 Q_{66}) s^2 c^2 + Q_{22} s^4 \\ q_{12L} = (Q_{11} + Q_{22} - 4 Q_{66}) s^2 c^2 + Q_{12} (s^4 + c^4) \\ q_{22L} = Q_{11} s^4 + 2 (Q_{12} + 2 Q_{66}) s^2 c^2 + Q_{22} c^4 \\ q_{16L} = (Q_{11} - Q_{12} - 2 Q_{66}) s c^3 + (Q_{12} - Q_{22} + 2 Q_{66}) s^3 c \\ q_{26L} = (Q_{11} - Q_{12} - 2 Q_{66}) s^3 c + (Q_{12} - Q_{22} + 2 Q_{66}) s c^3 \\ q_{66L} = (Q_{11} + Q_{22} - 2 Q_{12} - 2 Q_{66}) s^2 c^2 + Q_{66} (s^4 + c^4) \\ q_{44L} = Q_{44} c^2 + Q_{55} s^2, \quad q_{45L} = (Q_{55} - Q_{44}) s c, \quad q_{55L} = Q_{55} c^2 + Q_{44} s^2\end{split}\]

In matrix form, \(C^{(k)}\) and \(C_s^{(k)}\) of ply \(k\) are

(6)#\[\begin{split}C^{(k)} = \begin{bmatrix} q_{11L} & q_{12L} & q_{16L} \\ q_{12L} & q_{22L} & q_{26L} \\ q_{16L} & q_{26L} & q_{66L} \end{bmatrix}^{(k)}, \qquad C_s^{(k)} = \begin{bmatrix} q_{44L} & q_{45L} \\ q_{45L} & q_{55L} \end{bmatrix}^{(k)}\end{split}\]

relating \(\{\sigma_{xx}, \sigma_{yy}, \tau_{xy}\}\) to \(\{\varepsilon_{xx}, \varepsilon_{yy}, \gamma_{xy}\}\) and \(\{\tau_{yz}, \tau_{xz}\}\) to \(\{\gamma_{yz}, \gamma_{xz}\}\); Lamina.get_constitutive_matrix() returns both blocks in one 5 x 5 matrix. The terms \(q_{16L}\), \(q_{26L}\) and \(q_{45L}\) are odd functions of \(\theta\) and vanish at 0 and 90 degrees, the other terms are even; this is what makes some laminate stiffnesses vanish in balanced, orthotropic or symmetric laminates, see Laminate stiffness and plate theories.

The transformation of the stresses from the laminate to the material axes, e.g. to evaluate a failure criterion, is Lamina.get_transf_matrix_stress_to_lamina(), with \(\sigma_{11} = c^2 \sigma_{xx} + s^2 \sigma_{yy} + 2 s c \, \tau_{xy}\) as its first row, and its inverse is Lamina.get_transf_matrix_stress_to_laminate(), the same matrix for \(-\theta\); both are 6 x 6 in the order \(\{11, 22, 33, 23, 13, 12\}\). The displacements transform with the rotation matrix of Lamina.get_transf_matrix_displ_to_lamina().

Material invariants#

Written with the multiple angles \(2\theta\) and \(4\theta\), the stiffnesses of Eq. (5) are linear combinations of five constants of the material that do not depend on the ply angle, the invariants of Tsai and Pagano (Jones [2], Sec. 2.7, Eq. (2.99)), completed by two transverse shear invariants [4]:

(7)#\[\begin{split}u_1 = \frac{3 Q_{11} + 3 Q_{22} + 2 Q_{12} + 4 Q_{66}}{8}, \quad u_2 = \frac{Q_{11} - Q_{22}}{2}, \quad u_3 = \frac{Q_{11} + Q_{22} - 2 Q_{12} - 4 Q_{66}}{8} \\ u_4 = \frac{Q_{11} + Q_{22} + 6 Q_{12} - 4 Q_{66}}{8}, \quad u_5 = \frac{u_1 - u_4}{2}, \quad u_6 = \frac{Q_{44} + Q_{55}}{2}, \quad u_7 = \frac{Q_{44} - Q_{55}}{2}\end{split}\]

the attributes u1 to u7 of MatLamina, such that

(8)#\[\begin{split}q_{11L} = u_1 + u_2 \cos 2\theta + u_3 \cos 4\theta, \quad q_{22L} = u_1 - u_2 \cos 2\theta + u_3 \cos 4\theta \\ q_{12L} = u_4 - u_3 \cos 4\theta, \quad q_{66L} = u_5 - u_3 \cos 4\theta \\ q_{16L} = \frac{u_2}{2} \sin 2\theta + u_3 \sin 4\theta, \quad q_{26L} = \frac{u_2}{2} \sin 2\theta - u_3 \sin 4\theta \\ q_{44L} = u_6 + u_7 \cos 2\theta, \quad q_{45L} = -u_7 \sin 2\theta, \quad q_{55L} = u_6 - u_7 \cos 2\theta\end{split}\]

The coefficients of this relation are the 9 x 5 matrix of MatLamina.get_invariant_matrix(), whose columns multiply \(\{1, \cos 2\theta, \sin 2\theta, \cos 4\theta, \sin 4\theta\}\). Integrated through the thickness, the trigonometric terms become the lamination parameters, and the laminate stiffnesses become linear in them, see Lamination parameters and double-double laminates. For an isotropic material \(u_2 = u_3 = u_7 = 0\), and the ply angle has no effect.

Trace and trace normalization#

The trace of the plane-stress stiffness matrix of a ply, \(\mathrm{tr} = Q_{11} + Q_{22} + 2 Q_{66}\) (Melo et al. [5], Eq. (1)), does not depend on the ply angle: from Eq. (8), \(q_{11L} + q_{22L} + 2 q_{66L} = 2 (u_1 + u_5)\). MatLamina.trace_normalize_plane_stress() divides q11, q12, q22, q44, q55, q66 and u1 to u7 by the trace, in place, which makes them dimensionless. Since the laminate stiffnesses of Lamination parameters and double-double laminates are linear in the invariants, those computed with the trace-normalized invariants are the dimensional ones divided by \(\mathrm{tr}\); this is the basis of the trace-based design of Tsai and of the double-double laminates. The normalization requires the plane-stress constants and a positive trace, i.e. it is refused after rebuild(plane_stress=False) and for an antiplane core.
